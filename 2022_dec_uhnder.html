Senior System Engineer – Radar Feature Development, Uhnder Inc.

Summary

In December 2022, I was promoted to Senior System Engineer at Uhnder, recognizing my contributions to radar feature development and my focus on tackling radar interference. My role shifted toward enhancing radar performance under interference conditions through algorithm development, a key feature of our radar systems. Interference mitigation is a long-term, ongoing project critical to ensuring radar systems perform reliably even in the most challenging environments.

Key Contributions

  • Radar Interference Mitigation: Focused on addressing radar interference, which can obscure target visibility due to an elevated noise floor caused by external signals. The solution is a two-step process: sensing interference in the environment and mitigating its impact to lower the noise floor.
  • Prototype Development: Developed five radar interference management prototypes using MATLAB and Python. These prototypes formed the foundation for embedded interference management algorithms.
  • Algorithm Integration: Integrated interference sensing, classification, and mitigation algorithms into an embedded C++ radar system. These algorithms needed to run alongside the radar system at a strict 20 Hz, with significant memory constraints.
  • Automation & Testing: Implemented automated system-level testing for interference sensing and mitigation APIs, utilizing Bitbucket Pipelines to streamline the process. The introduction of key performance indicators (KPIs) improved deployment efficiency and accuracy.
  • Data-Driven Improvements: Established a robust testing and analytics framework using real radar data and simulations, leading to a tenfold improvement in interference resilience and a recovery of over 20 dB SNR in high-noise environments.

Challenges

One of the most complex challenges was balancing the need for high-speed performance with the strict memory and processing constraints of embedded radar systems. Interference mitigation algorithms had to operate seamlessly at 20 Hz without impacting the system's core functions, requiring careful optimization.
